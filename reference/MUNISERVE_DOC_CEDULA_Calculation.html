How CEDULA is Calculated

A simple guide for LGU staff on how the Community Tax Certificate (CEDULA) amount is automatically computed by the MuniServe system.

📋 Reference: Republic Act 7160 (Local Government Code) 📅 Applies to: All Business Permit Applications
1
What is a CEDULA?

A Community Tax Certificate (CEDULA) is an official document that proves a person or business has paid their community tax to the local government.

In the business permit process, the CEDULA is a required document before your application can proceed. It must be secured from the Treasurer's Office.

The amount you pay for your CEDULA depends on your organization type and your declared income or gross sales.

⚡ Automated: MuniServe automatically calculates the CEDULA amount based on the information the applicant submitted. The Treasurer reviews and confirms the amount before the applicant is asked to pay.

3
Formula — Individual (Sole Proprietorship)
Formula
CEDULA = ₱5.00 basic fee
          + ₱1.00 for every ₱1,000 of income (or fraction)
          Maximum: ₱5,005.00
💡 "Fraction" means that even if the remaining amount is less than ₱1,000, it is still counted as one full ₱1,000. For example, ₱1,500 = 2 counts of ₱1,000.
Example A — New Application
Sole Proprietor, New Application, Capital Investment = ₱50,000
Basic Fee₱5.00
Additional (₱50,000 ÷ ₱1,000 = 50 counts × ₱1.00)₱50.00
Total CEDULA Amount₱55.00
Example B — Renewal Application
Sole Proprietor, Renewal, Total Gross Sales = ₱500,000
Basic Fee₱5.00
Additional (₱500,000 ÷ ₱1,000 = 500 counts × ₱1.00)₱500.00
Total CEDULA Amount₱505.00
Example C — High Income (Cap Applied)
Sole Proprietor, Renewal, Total Gross Sales = ₱10,000,000
Basic Fee₱5.00
Additional (₱10,000,000 ÷ ₱1,000 = 10,000 counts × ₱1.00)₱10,000.00
⚠️ Maximum cap applied—
Total CEDULA Amount (capped)₱5,005.00
4
Formula — Juridical (Corporation / Partnership)
Formula
CEDULA = ₱500.00 basic fee
          + ₱2.00 for every ₱5,000 of gross receipts (or fraction)
          Maximum: ₱10,500.00
💡 Same "fraction" rule applies. ₱6,000 = 2 counts of ₱5,000.
Example D — New Corporation
Corporation, New Application, Capital Investment = ₱200,000
Basic Fee₱500.00
Additional (₱200,000 ÷ ₱5,000 = 40 counts × ₱2.00)₱80.00
Total CEDULA Amount₱580.00
Example E — Renewal Corporation
Corporation, Renewal, Total Gross Receipts = ₱2,000,000
Basic Fee₱500.00
Additional (₱2,000,000 ÷ ₱5,000 = 400 counts × ₱2.00)₱800.00
Total CEDULA Amount₱1,300.00

What Income Figure is Used?

The income figure used in the calculation depends on whether the application is New or a Renewal:

Application Type Organization Type Income Basis Used
New Sole Proprietor Capital Investment
New Corporation / Partnership Capital Investment
Renewal Sole Proprietor Total Gross Sales (previous year)
Renewal Corporation / Partnership Total Gross Receipts (previous year)
New Cooperative (CDA-registered) Capital Investment (treated as Juridical)
Renewal Cooperative (CDA-registered) Total Gross Receipts (treated as Juridical)
7
Cooperatives — What Are They Exempt From?
⚠️ Common Misconception: Cooperatives are often mistakenly believed to be exempt from CEDULA. This is incorrect. Their exemption only covers Local Business Tax (LBT) and other local taxes — not the Community Tax.

📋

Cooperatives still pay CEDULA — as a Juridical entity

Under Sections 156–162 of RA 7160 (Local Government Code), cooperatives are juridical persons engaged in business and are therefore required to pay the Community Tax. The exemption under RA 9520 Article 61 and LGC Section 133(n) covers local business taxes only — it does not extend to the Community Tax, which is governed by a separate chapter of the LGC entirely.


Fee / Tax Cooperative Exempt? Legal Basis
Local Business Tax (LBT) ✅ YES — Exempt RA 9520 Art. 61(3); LGC Sec. 133(n); BLGF MC 01-2020
Mayor's Permit Required (zero LBT cost) BLGF MC 01-2020
CEDULA / Community Tax ❌ NOT Exempt — Must pay LGC Sec. 156–162 (no cooperative exemption exists)

9
Legal Basis

Republic Act 7160 — Local Government Code of 1991
Sections 156 to 164 govern the Community Tax (CEDULA). These sections define who is required to pay, how the tax is computed, and the maximum amounts allowed.


Republic Act 9520 — Philippine Cooperative Code of 2008
Grants cooperatives full exemption from the community tax, among other tax privileges.


The amounts and formulas in this document are based on national law and apply uniformly to all LGUs. LGUs do not have the authority to change these rates.
